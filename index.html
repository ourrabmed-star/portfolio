Case study / MedSeg-MRI

Making model performance reviewable.

The dashboard turns a locked-validation result into a traceable research artifact. It separates overview performance from target-level detail, case-level exploration, epoch selection, and QA evidence.

End-to-end pipeline

01Source dataFour co-registered MRI modalities and discrete tumor labels from Task01_BrainTumour.
02Training systemFour-input, four-output MONAI 3D U-Net with deterministic preprocessing and guarded recovery.
03Evaluation layerFull-volume sliding-window inference, Dice, HD95, 1-mm surface Dice, precision, recall, and bootstrap intervals.
04Evidence productPower BI model, QA checks, model-selection evidence, release manifest, and research-only reporting.

Technical story

From MRI volumes to a defensible model decision.

The details below are taken from the verified project release documents and dashboard evidence.

01

Data and preprocessing

Each case uses four co-registered NIfTI channels ordered FLAIR, T1w, T1gd, and T2w. The verified workflow converts to channel-first, reorients images and labels to RAS, resamples to 1.25 mm isotropic spacing, uses bilinear interpolation for images and nearest-neighbor interpolation for labels, then applies nonzero per-channel MRI normalization.

484 labeled cases387 train / 97 locked validationlabels 0–3
02

Training and architecture

The model is a MONAI 3D U-Net with 4 input channels, 4 output classes, channels 16–256, four downsampling strides, two residual units, and 4,811,129 parameters. Training used DiceCELoss, AdamW, learning rate 0.0001, weight decay 1e-05, batch size 1 case, four sampled patches per case, and a 128 × 128 × 96 ROI.

PyTorch + MONAIAdamW3D segmentation
03

Recovery and model selection

AMP runs became numerically unstable after epoch 16. Nonfinite gradients and logits were detected, unsafe checkpoints were rejected, and training was recovered with preserved finite checkpoints and unscaled FP32. Epoch 19 completed without reported numerical errors and was selected; epoch 20 was rejected after validation Dice collapsed to 0.2265.

epoch 19 selectedepoch 20 rejectedcheckpoint hash verified
04

Inference and evaluation

Validation uses full-volume sliding-window inference with a 128 × 128 × 96 ROI, overlap 0.25, constant blending, and constant padding. The evaluation covers all 97 locked-validation cases and reports class and BraTS-style region metrics, deterministic 95% bootstrap intervals, HD95 in millimetres, and 1-mm surface Dice.

Scope and limitations

Verified results, boundaries, and next steps.

Verified

Epoch 19 is the selected research checkpoint. Whole Tumor Dice is 0.8944, foreground Dice is 0.6740, 36 QA checks passed, and spatial-export checks passed on the documented representative cases.

Not established

The 97-case set is locked validation, not independent testing. The provided test images are unlabeled, there is no external clinical validation, and the model must not be used for diagnosis or treatment decisions.

Next steps

Correct the stale root training summary, package the original training and inference source into a reproducible repository, add automated tests and CI, expose a reviewed service boundary if needed, and validate prospectively across sites, scanners, populations, and acquisition protocols.

Turning fragmented device intelligence into one risk view.

RegIntel 360 consolidates regulatory records, CAPA activity, complaints, biomedical maintenance, and audit findings into a repeatable data-processing workflow and interactive Power BI reporting layer. How it was built

One workflow, five operational lenses.

The project evidence includes the ETL notebook, Power BI dataset, architecture documentation, schema notes, and dashboard screenshots.

01

ETL and data quality

The notebook creates raw CSV sources, standardizes column names, cleans text fields, removes duplicate rows, exports processed datasets, and writes ETL and data-quality reports. The quality gate targets 99% completeness and zero duplicate rows.

02

Star-schema data model

The model separates shared dimensions from domain facts: Dim_Device and Dim_Date connect to regulatory, CAPA, complaints, maintenance, and audits. A consolidated Fact_Device_Risk table supports device-level monitoring and executive prioritization.

03

Risk engine

Regulatory, CAPA, complaint, maintenance, and audit signals are normalized and combined into component scores. The overall score uses weights of 25%, 25%, 20%, 15%, and 15%, then classifies devices as Critical, High, Medium, or Low.

04

Exploratory ML extension

A Random Forest experiment uses a binary high-risk target derived from risk_score ≥ 50 and 12 risk-related features. The documented test set contains only nine observations, so this is presented as an exploratory extension rather than validated future-event prediction.

Turning public-works work orders into traceable workflow insight.

This prototype applies a reproducible service-workflow analytics pipeline to the NYC Department of Environmental Protection Work Order Management Module. It profiles 508,243 work-order rows, preserves the source fields and records, and prepares supported operational measures for Power BI. The supplied NYC data is environmental and public works data, not biomedical equipment service data.

508,243source rows preserved
465,312eligible duration rows
743conflicting ID groups flagged

Project status: complete. This case study presents the delivered work at portfolio level and separates observed NYC DEP measures from external biomedical research context.

Data and delivery pipeline

01Inspect the sourceRead the local NYC DEP CSV, confirm its eight fields and timestamp format, and profile all 508,243 records.
02Audit and preserveFlag missing, out-of-range, reversed, and inconsistent timestamps plus repeated/conflicting work-order IDs. Keep every row; do not merge duplicate IDs.
03Calculate supported measuresTrend row volume by WORKORDER_ADDED, compare activity types, report completion-date coverage, and calculate start-to-completion hours only for valid, nonnegative timestamp pairs.
04Prepare the BI handoffDelivered the row-preserving Power BI import, profile and quality report, KPI definitions, query/DAX guidance, and report layout. The portfolio presents the project as complete per the owner confirmation and does not claim a .pbix unless one is included in the release.

What the data supports

Operational measures with visible boundaries.

Metrics are based on this extract and stated filters, not inferred service targets.

Observed workflow

Added-date volume spans December 1998 through June 2026; July 2025 is the highest month with 5,499 work-order rows. Activity counts use the recorded code and description.

Completion and duration

Completion timestamps are present on 92.41% of records. Among eligible start-to-completion intervals, median duration is 0.5 hours, P90 is 6 hours, and P95 is about 145.66 hours. Missing completion does not confirm an order is open.

Quality review

40,747 start dates and 38,571 completion dates are missing. 570 paired intervals end before they start. Date-range flags identify 23 start dates and 3 completion dates outside 1900 through the extract date. The duration distribution has a long tail and is shown with percentiles.

Source boundary

The NYC DEP file contains public-works orders and has no biomedical equipment ID, device downtime, agreed SLA due-date/target fields, customer-satisfaction responses, or validated first-response event. The dashboard’s local findings remain specific to those city work orders. Biomedical equipment availability, repeat equipment failures, SLA performance, customer satisfaction, and response-time KPIs are unavailable. WORKORDER_ADDED to WORKORDER_STARTED is not labeled response time; the timestamps are chronologically inconsistent on many rows.

Related research · separate hospital datasets

Honduran public hospitals

A study of 8,476 pieces of equipment across 25 hospitals reported 16.3% in poor condition and 9.2% requiring accessories; eight biomedical staff rated usability at 91.88/100. The authors combined an inventory/CMMS, dashboard, and WebGIS. Read the study ↗

Burundi CMMS evaluation

A 2017–2020 deployment reported average corrective-maintenance delays falling from 106 to 26 days and problem-resolution time from 106 to 32 days. The implementation also included asset standards, maintenance teams, workshops, training, and local support; the findings describe the combined program, not software alone. Read the peer-reviewed evaluation ↗

These studies provide external design and evaluation context for a future biomedical data track. Their equipment, hospital, response-time, and usability results are not measures of the NYC DEP project.

Duration eligibility requires parseable start and completion timestamps within the documented 1900-01-01 to 2026-11-24 range and completion not earlier than start. The date-range rule is an analytical filter, not proof that an out-of-range source value is false.

Shared data model

Department, site and location, part catalogue, supplier, equipment, inventory balance, request and request line, stock transaction, replenishment, user-role assignment, and approval history.

Controlled operations

Receipts, issues, returns, two-sided transfers, and reasoned authorized adjustments create ledger entries. On-hand stock reconciles to opening count plus and minus recorded movements.

Technology decision

Power Apps for role-aware screens; Power Automate for approval and alert flows; SharePoint Lists for a suitably scoped MVP or Dataverse if relational and row-level security needs justify it. Confirm licensing, scale, and access rules before choosing.

Management measures

Completed research synthesis / MIRA-RT

Compare MR-guided radiotherapy platforms through their evidence.

MIRA-RT (MR-guided Imaging & Adaptive Radiotherapy) is a focused literature synthesis of hybrid MRI–linear accelerator systems. It compares published MRIdian (0.35 T) and Elekta Unity (1.5 T) configurations, online adaptation and motion-management workflows, magnetic-field effects on dose, and clinical evidence for mobile abdominal targets.

Project status: completed research synthesis, version 0.2. The deliverable includes a platform comparison, descriptive indication/evidence matrix, glossary, limitations, and open research questions.

What the synthesis covers

01Platform comparisonPublished field strengths, accelerator configurations, imaging, tracking, gating, adaptation, and dose calculation.
02Magnetic-field dosimetryLorentz-force effects, electron return at tissue interfaces, and the need for configuration-specific commissioning and QA.
03Clinical evidencePancreatic and liver MR-guided workflows, alongside selected prostate, lung, registry, and cross-platform evidence.
04Evidence boundariesSeparates reported study findings from interpretation; highlights heterogeneity, workflow burden, and unanswered comparative questions.

External evidence used in the synthesis

Cross-platform dosimetric comparison

A 2025 retrospective planning study recreated 20 prostate and pancreas cases across MRIdian and Unity. Most organ-at-risk metrics did not differ significantly; selected differences were site-specific. Dosimetric planning comparison does not establish general clinical superiority.

Read the open-access study ↗
Prospective pancreatic SMART evidence

A multicentre, single-arm phase II study reports outcomes for selected patients treated with MR-guided adaptive radiotherapy. Its design supports feasibility and prospective outcome reporting, but does not provide a randomized platform comparison.

View the study record ↗
High-field clinical safety cohort

A multicentre cohort describes acute toxicity across 1,793 treatment courses on a 1.5 T MR-guided system, including an online-adaptive subgroup. These observational results are contextual evidence, not proof of comparative benefit.

Read the cohort study ↗
Magnetic-field dosimetry guidance

Professional guidance summarizes measurement and quality-assurance considerations for MRI-linac dosimetry, including the effects that require system-specific commissioning.

Read the dosimetry position paper ↗

Evidence boundary: these external publications support the scientific context of the synthesis; they are not measurements or outcomes produced by MIRA-RT. This targeted review is not a systematic review or GRADE assessment and makes no claim that one platform is clinically superior. The source document’s references [80]–[92] were not supplied, so those numbered citations are not claimed as verified; the synthesis uses its own traceable references.
